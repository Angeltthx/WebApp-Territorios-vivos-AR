# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## What this is

A browser-based WebAR experience (image tracking, not markerless/world tracking) that works on iPhone Safari and Android Chrome with no app install. Uses **MindAR** for computer-vision tracking (WebXR is not an option — Safari on iPhone doesn't support it) and **Three.js** for rendering.

The marker is an illustrated map of Nuquí, Chocó (`public/targets/map.jpg`). **Seeing the map is not enough to see the animals** — it draws a **gold dashed outline that beats** around each of the five (whale, guan/pava, crab, turtle, harlequin frog), traced from how that animal is *drawn*, and the 3D model only materializes, with a puff of smoke, when you **tap** that one (dormant outlines are tappable: their tap zone is the outline itself) — one tap opens it; in the close-up, each tap plays its gesture. Discovering it **unlocks it for the rest of the session** (walking away never hides it again) and opens its card — name, a scientific / common-name line, and every paragraph of text the team delivered for it — closed with an X. **While an animal is in focus, the focus is locked**: tapping another animal does nothing until the X, and closing opens nothing by itself. In focus it **sounds**: its habitat plays in a loop (sea, mangrove beach, reef, Chocó forest) and one of its real recorded calls plays on entry and another, different one every 10–16 s. **Once all five animals have been opened, a yellow pulsing marker appears on each of the map's printed texts**, and the tutorial's hand moves on to them («Toca los puntos amarillos»). The markers sit on the tourism paragraphs, the directory of local businesses, the pink place labels, the town names and the website. Tapping one opens it big for reading over the dimmed camera, no card behind it: the SAME text, transcribed, in the map's own styles, justified, with up to three REAL photos of the place above it (the Emberá tambos, the dance company, Chachita, the mangrove…) and the sound of the place it talks about. As soon as the map is found, a **tutorial** starts: a hand (`TapHint`, a camera-facing sprite whose fingertip rests on the animal, gold ripples on the paper at each press) taps the whale under «Toca un animal»; when its narration goes quiet another hand points at the ✕; on closing, the hand moves to another animal still to open, and finally to one text point and a farewell; each step insists after 6 s (bigger, faster hand; the title beats). See the `Tutorial` gotcha. **Tapping the animal in focus plays its gesture, and its tap sound comes at the moment of the gesture that makes it** (a blow, a splash, a scuttle — different from its calls) and plays its gesture — the whale breaches and dives, the turtle leaps out and falls back in, the crab scuttles, the guan sings, the frog tapped jumps at once and lands back on its leaf (there are two frogs, a big one and a small one, both outlined, both in the close-up); tapping again mid-gesture does nothing until it finishes, and the animal never changes size. On the map a tap always OPENS (it no longer plays the gesture there).

Opening the link shows the **splash screen** (`#boot`) the company's designer delivered — one image carrying the title, the photo, the partner logos and a drawn **«Iniciar experiencia AR»** button — and it stays until that button is tapped; only then is the camera requested. That tap matters twice over: a system permission prompt over a page nobody asked for gets denied, and iOS only unlocks audio (and stops treating the camera feed as a player) inside a real user gesture. An earlier version started by itself after 4 s; the design brought the button back, and the button brought the gesture back with it. Three overlays carry the instructions in order: `#boot`, then `#guide` (viewfinder frame in the map's exact 1000:1432 proportion, "Apunta al mapa de Nuquí"), then `#approach` ("Acerca tu cámara a un animal"). The buttons left are **«?»** (top right), which brings the guide back for 5 s; **☰** (top left), a round translucent twin of it that drops down a semi-transparent menu — three options («Historia», «Redes sociales», «Próximamente»), wired to nothing yet; and **Reintentar**, which appears solely on error — without it, denying the camera by accident would kill the session until reload. «?» shows once the session has started and hides while an animal's card is open (it would collide with the X). ☰ shows once the session has started and STAYS with a card open — its dropdown starts with the **volume** control («Sonido»: a round speaker that mutes, a gold slider for the level, remembered in `localStorage` under `territorios-vivos:volumen`, applied through `AudioPort.setVolume` to the master bus) and the card is exactly when the narration plays; it hides only while a map text is open. Dragging turns **one** animal in place — the one under the finger when the drag starts, or the one in focus (then from anywhere on screen) — sideways *and* up/down (pitch capped at ±70°); starting a drag on empty map turns nothing. It used to be one `rotationY` for all four, so dragging the whale turned the other three too. **There is no pinch-to-scale** — it was removed on request, because each animal's size is calibrated in the catalog and hand-scaling broke that hierarchy. The five icons are `.glb` models made by the team's 3D designer, in `public/models/`; the sounds are real recordings in `public/audio/`, built by `npm run build-audio` (see below); the old Web Audio synthesis (`sound` in the catalog) remains the fallback for an animal without recordings. `PrimitiveFactory` still builds a procedural stand-in for every animal — switch a catalog entry back to `ModelSource.primitive(...)` and it runs with no assets at all.

## Commands

```bash
npm install            # slow: pulls in MindAR + TensorFlow.js
npm run dev             # vite --host over HTTPS (required for camera off localhost)
VITE_HTTP=1 npm run dev # same, no TLS — desktop-only inspection (see /verify.html)
npm run build           # vite build
npm run preview         # vite preview --host
npm run typecheck       # tsc --noEmit
npm run prepare-target  # "Mapa para el fondo.jpeg" -> public/targets/map.jpg
npm run compile-target  # public/targets/map.jpg -> public/targets/map.mind (+ map.mind.gz)
npm run optimize-models # models-src/*.glb -> public/models/*.glb (webp + draco)
npm run optimize-splash # "Pantalla Inicial.jpeg" -> public/splash/inicio.{webp,jpg}
npm run make-icons      # LOGO.png -> public/icons/* + public/favicon.ico (tab, Google, iPhone)
npm run build-audio     # audio-src/{sources,clips}.json -> public/audio/<animal>/*.mp3 (+ narration-en-*) + CREDITOS.md
npm run make-photos     # photos-src/photos.json + ../webdoc/Contenido -> public/photos/<text>-<n>.webp
npm run bench-detection # measures how well a .mind survives glare (no camera)
npm test                # tests/regressions.test.ts, bundled by Vite and run with node:test
```

There is no lint script. `npm test` covers domain rules, the animator, the tap
choreographies and the pin/adapter wiring (no WebGL needed). For detection,
`npm run bench-detection` is the tool: it runs MindAR's real `Detector` + `Matcher` against synthetic
frames of the map under increasing glare and distance, and reports both hits and
**inlier counts**. Compare two `.mind` files with it before believing any claim
that detection got better — pass/fail alone flips on noise near the threshold,
the inlier total is the signal.

`npm run dev` prints a LAN address (`https://192.168.x.x:5173`); open that from the phone, not `localhost`. Safari will warn about the self-signed cert — accept via *Show Details → Visit this website*. Debugging requires the physical device: Safari Web Inspector (from a Mac) for iPhone, `chrome://inspect` for Android.

### Verifying without a phone

`/verify.html` (dev-only — `vite build` bundles `index.html` only) mounts **the real `MarkerPin` objects** onto a picture of the map, placed in the exact anchor plane and scale MindAR uses. It reveals all five after 2.5 s, so the first seconds show the dashed outlines and the rest shows the models; `?reveal=999999` freezes the outlines for measuring them, and a short value lets you catch the smoke. It checks shape, placement and colour, but **not** movement: a headless/background tab has `requestAnimationFrame` frozen (`document.visibilityState === 'hidden'`), so every screenshot of it comes out identical and the beat has to be judged on a real screen. The orthographic view has no perspective to hide a mistake: if an outline doesn't sit on its animal, the catalog coordinates are wrong. Run `VITE_HTTP=1 npm run dev` and open it — the self-signed cert otherwise blocks headless/automated inspection. Clicking an animal in the flat view plays its tap gesture. Because rAF freezes in a background tab, the page exposes three console helpers for inspecting motion anyway: `tap('whale')` starts a gesture, `stepVerify(1.9)` advances every pin by exactly that many seconds and renders — so a screenshot catches the whale mid-jump or the splash on the frame it fires — and `measureIcons()` returns each animal's on-screen width in map widths (take a screenshot first so the reveal has actually rendered).

`/verify-stage.html` (dev-only too) is the same idea for the **focused close-up**: it mounts the real `ThreeSceneAdapter` on a fake runtime (camera at the origin looking down −Z, like MindAR) with a real WebGL renderer, so what you see is the real lighting, tone mapping, environment map and three-quarter framing, and the drag is the real `PointerInteractionAdapter`. Buttons switch animals; `?animal=crab` opens one; `?zoom=2` enlarges the phone and its resolution to judge the light; `stepStage(s)` advances time; `stageAdapter`/`stageScene` are exposed. Judge light changes there with a before/after screenshot, and measure framing in pixels (read the canvas alpha right after `stepStage`), not by eye.

`node scripts/preview-spots.mjs '[{"u":0.26,"v":0.22}]' out.jpg` draws crosshairs on the map at given normalized coordinates — use it to measure a new `spot` before putting it in the catalog.

`node scripts/trace-outline.mjs whale ascii` traces an animal **as it is drawn on the map** and prints it as an ASCII picture; `… whale poly 22` prints the same thing as the `outlineShape` block the catalog expects. **Always look at `ascii` first**: the trace is a per-animal colour rule plus the largest matching blob, so if the rule leaks into the background the outline comes out enormous and the ASCII shows it instantly.

## Architecture

Hexagonal (ports and adapters). The single rule: **dependencies point inward.** Read `README.md` for the full rationale (in Spanish) — the essentials:

```
src/
├── domain/            Pure rules, zero external imports.
│   ├── entities/       ArModel, ArSession, Placement
│   └── value-objects/  ModelId, Scale, Vector3, MarkerSpot, ModelSource, SoundProfile, Stabilization, IconPose, Discovery, Language, AnimationSequence, Soundscape, MapText
├── application/
│   ├── ports/          TrackingPort, ScenePort, AudioPort, InteractionPort, ModelRepository, AnalyticsPort
│   └── use-cases/       StartArExperience, TransformPlacement, TapModel, PlayGestureSound, DiscoverModel, Tutorial, CloseFocus, AnimalSoundscape, OpenMapText
├── infrastructure/     MindAR and Three.js live ONLY here.
│   ├── mindar/          MindArRuntime (shared instance, owns the render loop) + FrameLockedBackground
│   ├── tracking/        MindArTrackingAdapter
│   ├── rendering/       ThreeSceneAdapter, MarkerPin, IconLoader, IconAnimator, TapChoreography, WaterSplash, MapTextHotspot, TapHint, IconSilhouette, SmokePuff, ThreePointLighting, PrimitiveFactory
│   ├── interaction/      PointerInteractionAdapter (raycast + gestures)
│   ├── audio/            WebAudioAdapter (recordings: clips + seamless ambience loop; synthesis fallback)
│   ├── repositories/    StaticModelRepository + NUQUI_CATALOG, StaticMapTextRepository + NUQUI_MAP_TEXTS
│   └── di/container.ts ← composition root
├── ui/                 ArView — paints state, emits intents; knows nothing about MindAR/Three.js; i18n.ts — every UI string, es/en
└── verify.ts           Dev-only coordinate check (see above)
```

- **`TrackingPort` is the port that justifies the whole architecture.** MindAR (unmaintained since Jan 2024, single maintainer) is the volatile piece. Swapping it for WebXR/Zappar/8th Wall means writing a new adapter and changing exactly two lines in `src/infrastructure/di/container.ts` (marked with arrows in the file) — domain, use cases, and UI stay untouched.
- `container.ts` is the **composition root**: the only place concrete implementations get wired to ports. Start here to see how everything connects.
- The 60fps render loop is deliberately **not** behind a port (abstracting per-frame matrices was judged not worth the cost); it lives in `MindArRuntime`, and adapters subscribe via `onFrame()`.
- **One tap opens an animal; in the close-up, one tap plays its gesture.** They used to reveal when the phone came close (a `Proximity` rule) — impractical and unintuitive. Then for a while ONE tap woke an animal and made it move and a DOUBLE tap opened it; it was dropped because nobody expects a double tap on a web page and the tutorial had to spend a whole step (and a «2×» badge) teaching it. Now `PointerInteractionAdapter` → `TapModel`, which routes: on the map it calls `DiscoverModel` (unlock + focus + sound, dormant outline or not), in the close-up it plays the gesture of the animal in front. `Discovery` still keeps **unlocked** (`isUnlocked`) and **opened** (`isOpened`, `openedCount`) apart even though a tap does both today: `hasFoundAll` (which turns the text markers on) and the tutorial count OPENED. All pins are pickable always; while dormant, a pin's tap ellipse is its outline (`MarkerPin.outlineExtent`). `ThreeSceneAdapter.measureUnit` still measures a map width in world units for the close-up.
- **Unlocked and focused are two different things, and `Discovery` (domain) keeps them apart.** Unlocked is forever: an animal you got close to stays on the map for the rest of the session even if you walk away, which is what makes exploring the map *advance* instead of repeat. Focused is momentary: the one being shown big with its card, closed by the X (`CloseFocus`) — and closing it hides nothing. Walking away from an animal is not an instruction, so it closes nothing either.
- **`MarkerSpot` (domain) + `MarkerPin` (infrastructure) are the pairing this design rests on.** `MarkerSpot` says *"the crab is at (0.487, 0.472) of the image"* — normalized 0–1 from the image's **top-left**, the way you'd measure on the image file. `MarkerPin` translates that into MindAR anchor coordinates and builds the outline, tap zone, smoke and floating icon. `MarkerPin` deliberately **does not depend on MindAR or the runtime**, which is exactly what lets `verify.html` mount the real pins without a camera.
- **Anchor coordinate system** — verified in MindAR's source, not guessed (`image-target/three.js:214-229` and the comment on `controller.js:_glModelViewMatrix`):
  - Origin is the **center** of the marker image; the image **width is 1 unit**, its height is `targetAspect`.
  - **+X** right, **+Y up** (MindAR flips the image's Y with `y' = h - y`), **+Z out of the paper toward the camera**.
  - The conversion lives in one function, `anchorPositionOf`: `x = u - 0.5`, `y = (0.5 - v) * targetAspect`.
- Scene graph hierarchy (needed to follow `ThreeSceneAdapter`):
  ```
  scene → follower (marker pose, SMOOTHED — content never hangs directly off MindAR's anchor)
            └── overlay (global offset)
                  └── MarkerPin ×4 (fixed position = its MarkerSpot)
                        ├── outline (dashed silhouette flat on the paper,
                        │     shown while the animal is still dormant)
                        ├── tap zone (invisible, generous)
                        ├── smoke (SmokePuff — sprites, fires once on reveal)
                        └── lift (floats along +Z, bobbing; height adapts
                              so a deep icon never sinks under the paper)
                              └── icon (spun and scaled in place)
  ```
  Content is deliberately not parented directly to MindAR's anchor because doing so inherits its raw frame-to-frame tracking jitter. `follower` copies the anchor pose every frame through `PoseFilter`, a **One Euro filter**: the cutoff rises with the *filtered* speed of the map (`Stabilization.restCutoffHz` + `linearResponse`·map widths/s + `angularResponse`·rad/s, one cutoff shared by position, rotation and scale so the animals never slide over the paper). A tremor goes back and forth, its filtered speed stays near zero, and the animals stay still; a sweep of the phone keeps its direction and opens the filter with no visible lag. It replaced a per-frame snap threshold (smooth below `SNAP_DISTANCE`/`SNAP_ANGLE` per frame, follow outright above), which let the vibration through on noisy phones and shaky hands — a single bad frame crossed the threshold — so the animals "vibrated depending on the device". `npm test` checks a 5 Hz tremor is cut to under 30% and a 1 map-width/s sweep lags under 0.06. The base level is fixed at `Stabilization.default()` — the Fast/Balanced/Stable button was removed from the UI, but `StartArExperience.applyStabilization` is still there if a control is ever wanted back.
- **Rotation and scale never move an icon off its animal.** `Placement.orientationOf(id)` (yaw + pitch, per animal) and `Placement.scale` spin, tilt and resize each icon *in place*; pitch is applied about the PARENT's X (Euler XYZ puts X last), so dragging down always tilts down whatever way the animal faces; a change that makes them displace the icons is a bug, not a feature. `Placement.offset` moves the whole overlay and is currently always zero.
- `ArSession` (in `domain/entities/ArSession.ts`) is a frozen state machine (`idle → preparing → searching → tracking/lost → error`) with guarded transitions — invalid states (e.g. `tracking()` without a prior `Placement`) throw. `TransformPlacement`/etc. read/write this via the use cases, never by mutating fields directly.
- Path aliases (`@domain`, `@application`, `@infrastructure`, `@ui`) are defined in both `tsconfig.json` and `vite.config.ts` — keep them in sync if either changes.

## Project-specific gotchas (read before touching related code)

- **Two languages, Spanish (the original) and English, chosen by the phone and switchable in the ☰ menu.** `detectLanguage(navigator.languages)` (domain, `Language.ts`): the first entry that is `es` or `en` wins, anything else → Spanish, the map's language. The ☰ menu has «Idioma / Language» (Español / English, each written in its own language so whoever can't read the other still finds it), remembered in `localStorage` under `territorios-vivos:idioma`; `ArView.setLanguage` repaints everything and fires `onLanguageChange` (also once at construction), which `main.ts` sends to `AnimalSoundscape.setLanguage` and keeps for `StartArExperience` (which narration parts to preload first). Where each text lives: every UI string in `src/ui/i18n.ts` (`STRINGS.es/en`; `Rich` = before/**gold**/after, painted with nodes, never HTML; session errors translated by `SessionErrorCode`, the Spanish message stays the source); animal cards in the catalog's `english` field (`ArModel.cardIn(lang)`); map texts in `NUQUI_MAP_TEXTS[].english` (`MapText.blocksIn/labelIn`) — only what translates: proper names (places, businesses, the web) stay as printed, a text with only proper names has no English version and shows the Spanish one. `document.documentElement.lang` follows the language, which also switches CSS hyphenation. The card and reading view re-render when the language changes (`data-card`/`data-reading` keys include it). **Not translatable from code:** the splash is one image with «Iniciar experiencia AR» drawn in; an English splash needs a second image from the designer.
- **The English narrations read the SAME card as Carolina, in an AI voice (ElevenLabs, «v4»), cut at the equivalent sentence.** They replaced the team's first English recordings, which said the older, longer text; the script given to the voice was each English card preceded by the name as Carolina says it (Humpback whale, Ghost crab or sand crab, Chocó guan, Hawksbill turtle), sentence for sentence with her. `audios/WHALE.mp3`, `CRAB.mp3`, `CHOCO GUAN.mp3`, `TORTLUE.mp3` (sic) → `clips.json` `narrationEn` → `build-audio` (`cutNarration`, shared with Spanish) → `public/audio/<animal>/narration-en-{1,2}.mp3`; catalog `soundscape.narrationEn`, same `cues` as Spanish (the animal acts at the same point of the story; `npm test` checks it). Cuts found like the Spanish ones: transcribed with faster-whisper (word timestamps) to find the sentence that invites the animal to act — «…the longest melody in the animal kingdom.», «…on legs the color of Earth.», «…the forest is healthy.», «…always remembers the way back.» — then placed mid-silence (`silencedetect` −42 dB): 17.38, 6.57, 10.27, 6.86 s; re-transcribing the parts confirmed every cut falls between sentences. `Soundscape.narrationIn(lang)` falls back to Spanish if a language has no recording. **Switching language mid-narration continues in the SAME sentence** (`npm test` checks it both ways, for every sentence of every part). If one language is ever re-recorded alone, the two stop saying the same thing: put the animal in `ENGLISH_NOT_YET_MATCHING` (tests) and drop the stale `sentences` — without them `resumePoint` restarts the part, which beats jumping to a sentence that is not the same; equal sentence COUNTS are not enough, they can match by chance. How it works: both recordings go sentence by sentence (same count per part; the Spanish guan's «endémica…, no existe en ningún otro planeta» is one sentence, so the English «…region. It lives nowhere else on the planet.» counts as one), each narration carries `sentences` — where every sentence of every part starts, measured with faster-whisper — and `resumePoint(from, to, part, seconds)` finds the sentence playing (`AudioPort.voicePosition()`) and returns where that same sentence starts in the other language; `playVoice(url, fromSeconds)` starts the part there with a 60 ms fade-in. Switching during a pause (the animal acting) or before the voice starts just makes the next part come out in the new language, because `narrate` looks the narration up by language on every part. `npm test` checks every Spanish/English part has the same number of sentences. Like Carolina's, the English narrations say «Chocó guan» and «Hawksbill turtle» while the cards use the poster's names.
- **The site icon is LOGO.png, and without it everything showed Netlify's** — the tab, Google results, and iOS «Now Playing» when the page sounds in the background. `npm run make-icons` cuts two families from it: trimmed to the rounded square (transparent corners) for favicons, and cropped INSIDE the rounded corners (opaque) for `apple-touch-icon` and the 512 icon, because iOS rounds on its own and fills transparent corners with black. `favicon.ico` is written by hand (an ICO with one PNG inside). `WebAudioAdapter.describeNowPlaying` sets `navigator.mediaSession.metadata` (title, «Nuquí, Chocó», the icons) on the first sound. Like the splash, the original stays in the repo root, unserved. **Icon URLs carry `?v=2`** (in `index.html`, the manifest and `describeNowPlaying`): browsers keep favicons in their own store and do NOT refresh them on reload, so whoever had seen the page with Netlify's icon kept seeing it after the logo shipped. Bump the number when the logo changes. `netlify.toml` serves the manifest as `application/manifest+json` (it went out as octet-stream).
- **MindAR assumes a 45° camera; phones see ~66°, and that is why the animals sat off their drawings on the printed poster.** `controller.js:38` fixes `fovy = 45°` for every camera and builds the focal length from it; it is used both to estimate the map's pose and to draw the scene. On-plane content still lands right with any focal (the plane homography is exact), but the tilt deduced from the map comes out wrong and anything FLOATING above the paper — every animal, the whale a lot — is drawn displaced, more the higher it floats and the more obliquely the phone looks. `CameraModel.installCameraModel` puts a setter on the MindARThree instance's `controller` property: `_startAR` does `this.controller = new Controller(…)` and immediately uses it (resize → the three camera's fov; `addImageTargets` → the tracker copies the focal), so the assignment is the only moment to fix it. The setter rewrites `projectionTransform` in place and `projectionMatrix` via the controller's own `_glProjectionMatrix` (both names survive minification; checked in `dist/controller-*.js`). Verified against the real bundle: a 720×1280 feed goes from focal 1545 px / 45° to 985 px / 66°. 66° on the long side is the phone default (iPhone main camera ≈ 69°, Androids 24–28 mm); on a non-touch device MindAR's 45° is kept (webcams); `?fov=70` in the URL overrides it for calibration with the poster in hand. **If animals still sit off their drawings when the phone looks straight at the poster, the print is not `map.jpg`** — then the fix is the final PDF: re-prepare, recompile, re-measure spots and outlines.
- **There is no focus-mode blur** (removed at the client's request). For a while the camera was blurred iPhone-portrait style (`body[data-focus]`) and the map layer hidden during focus; both went together, since the hiding only existed because sharp animals over a blurred background looked pasted on.
- **The tap hand is drawn for this app** (`HAND_SVG` in `TapHint`, loaded as a texture from a data URL): white, rounded, faint warm gradient, soft shadow, a gold glow on the fingertip. Material's `touch_app` came first and was rejected as a hard-edged computer cursor. Iterate on it by rendering the SVG with sharp over the map colours at 72 px as well as large — it is small on the phone.
- **The tutorial is ONE clock heard by two, and walks the user animal by animal** (`Tutorial`, application). For every animal not yet opened, the same cycle: `tapAnimal` — the hand taps an animal («Toca un animal»; the first time always the WHALE, `preferred`; later «Toca otro animal / Te faltan N»), and a tap opens it; the FIRST time each animal opens, `tapFocused`: a DOM hand taps the animal in the close-up (fingertip at 31 % of the screen height — the stage sits `STAGE_LIFT` above centre; lower, it covered the card's name on 320×568) with «Tócalo para ver su animación» at the top, under the buttons (`#focus-tap-hint`, `#focus[data-tap-hint]`, `gestureTaught`), gone at the first tap on it (`TapModel` → `Tutorial.focusedAnimalTapped`); when the narration goes quiet (it ended or the user stopped it — `AnimalSoundscape.onNarrationChange` now takes several listeners), `closeFocus`: a DOM hand in the card taps the ✕ from below, a gold ring beats around it and «Toca la ✕ para cerrar» (`#close-hint`, `#focus[data-close-hint]`; ONCE per session, with the first animal — per animal it became tedious by the third —, `closeTaught`); on closing an ANIMAL, immediately the next one. With all five opened, `tapText`: the hand taps ONE point («Toca un punto amarillo»); on closing that first text, `explore` — «Explora el resto de Nuquí / Toca los puntos amarillos que quieras», no hand, for 5 s (`TUTORIAL_FAREWELL_MS`) — and the tutorial is over (`finished`): guiding text by text through a dozen points was too much and took away the best of the ending, exploring freely. Every step turns `urgent` after 6 s. The scene (`ScenePort.setHint(HintSpec)` → `TapHint`) and the view (`ArView.setTutorial` → `#approach` text + `data-urgent`, and the ✕ hint) subscribe, so hand and banner move together. The scene picks WHICH candidate: the preferred one if visible, else the most centred; it keeps it while within ±0.85 of the screen and only moves to one the user APPROACHES — centred (< 0.28) AND closer than 0.85 map widths (`HINT_APPROACH_DISTANCE`), because with the whole map framed the screen centre falls near the crab. The hand never takes a tap. This replaced the «¿Quieres conocer más del Chocó?» card and its `RecurringNudge`, both removed.
- **Tap tolerance is generous but never steals.** Tap zones grew a little (`TAP_FOOTPRINT_MARGIN` 1.05 → 1.15; dormant outline × `DORMANT_TAP_MARGIN` 1.1) because on some phones a tap on an animal's edge did not register. On top of that, `PointerInteractionAdapter.forgivingTargetAt`: when the ray under the finger hits NOTHING, it samples rings at 10 and 18 px (8 directions each) and takes what most samples of the nearest ring hit; whatever is directly under the finger always wins, so a tolerance can never take a tap away from another animal or point.
- **Text markers are yellow, small and translucent** (`MapTextHotspot`). The warm-white 0.007 dot read as one more white label on a map full of them; a 0.012 opaque yellow dot with a white rim was the overcorrection — a dozen of them covered the illustration. Now radius 0.0095, dot at 72 % opacity, rim 55 %, halo 16 %, waves 45 % reaching 0.04.
- **The outline is gold and beats, and both were forced by the phone.** It started white (the same visual grammar as the "Apunta al mapa" viewfinder) with a 2% sine breath, and on the map nobody noticed it: the illustration is already full of white — the place names, the species labels, the surf, the clouds — so one more white line reads as part of the drawing, and a slow continuous movement is exactly the one the eye stops seeing after two seconds. Now: `OUTLINE_COLOUR` 0xffc53d, a colour the map does not contain, over the dark backgrounds all five animals sit on (turquoise sea for three, green jungle for the guan and the frog); thickness up from 0.004 to 0.0065 map widths; and a **heartbeat** instead of a breath — grows 11% and brightens over 0.31 s, returns by 0.62 s, then rests until the 2.4 s cycle comes round. The silence is what makes the next beat register. Each pin keeps its `phase`, so the five never beat in unison: synchronised they look like a screen effect, staggered they look alive. The guide viewfinder stays white on purpose — white frames the map, gold says there is something here to find.

- **Envelope floors, not `durationMs`, decide how long a sound is actually heard.** An exponential ramp down to ~0 crosses four decades: half way through it is already at ~1% of the peak, so a 220 ms profile was audible for about 20 ms and taps felt mute. The bus decays to 4% of peak (−28 dB) and the overtone gains to 10% of their weight — the two envelopes multiply, so both have to stay off zero. Verified by rendering the real adapter through an `OfflineAudioContext` and measuring: audible time roughly tripled (whale 231 → 726 ms, crab 23 → 75 ms) with peaks near 0.5 and no clipping.

- **MindAR's compiler and its runtime disagree about greyscale, and `scripts/compile-target.mjs` reconciles them.** `CompilerBase` flattens colour with a plain mean `(R+G+B)/3`, but on the phone `input-loader.js` converts each camera frame with Rec.601 luma (`0.299R + 0.587G + 0.114B`) in a shader. Compiled naively, the descriptors in the `.mind` describe an image the camera never produces — a costly mismatch on a map this saturated, where blue counts three times more in the compiler than on the device. The script pre-bakes luma into all three channels before handing pixels to the compiler, so the compiler's mean returns exactly the shader's signal. - **MindAR always inserts a CSS3D layer over the WebGL canvas, and it eats every tap.** `MindARThree` builds a `CSS3DRenderer` whether or not you use CSS anchors and appends it *after* the canvas (`three.js:42-43`), so its full-screen `<div>` sits on top. three sets `pointerEvents: 'none'` on the inner `viewElement` but **not** on that outer div, and a transparent div swallows pointer events exactly like an opaque one — `document.elementFromPoint()` at the screen centre returned the div, never the canvas, so the raycast never ran and tapping an animal did nothing at all. `MindArRuntime.configureRenderer` now sets `pointerEvents = 'none'` on it. It now runs `_startAR` on a blank canvas the size the camera frame will have, then disposes that controller (worker closes itself; the tracker's map tensors are released). TensorFlow keeps the compiled programs, keyed by shape, so the real start finds them (measured: `dummyRun` 2.2 s → 0.17 s).   **The `.mind` is downloaded during the splash, not after the tap.** MindAR fetches the target inside `start()`, *after* the camera permission (`_startVideo` first, `_startAR` second), so nearly 900 KB used to sit between "Allow" and the first detection attempt. `TrackingPort.prewarm()` is the port method for "get your downloads out of the way before the gesture"; `MindArRuntime.prefetchTarget` fetches the file and keeps it as a `blob:` URL that `init()` passes to MindAR instead of the path. Only with `wander` — the crab's `Walk` and the turtle's `Swin` are also their tap clips and part of their loop, but that is how they move, and tapping them mid-walk must answer. The tap clip is kept out of `fitToIconSize`'s measure even when it is in the loop. Spontaneous jumps are silent; the leaves sound on taps.
  - **Its texture is black with orange markings** (the drawing is the reverse: red with black spots). On the map, small and in the orthographic view, it reads almost black; in the close-up the three-point light and rim bring the orange out. That is the designer's texture, not a lighting bug — the lighting is the same `ThreePointLighting` + `addRimShading` every model gets through `IconLoader`.
  - **Sounds**: calls are *Oophaga pumilio* (same genus, CC BY 3.0 — there is no open recording of *O. solanensis*), the tap is leaves rustling where it lands (Gravity Sound, CC BY 4.0), the ambience is Chocó forest with the frog trill under it.
  - **No narration yet** and **provisional card text** (genus facts: aposematic colouring, alkaloids from ants and mites, the mother carrying tadpoles to bromeliads and feeding them eggs — hence *Oophaga*). Both wait for the team. `npm test` lists it in `WITHOUT_NARRATION` and fails the day it gets one, so the list gets cleaned up. Without a narration nothing «goes quiet», so the tutorial teaches the ✕ on its card as soon as its gesture is tapped (`Tutorial.narrationChanged(playing, available)`), still once per session.
- **Animal names are the POSTER's** (the strongest reference we have, per the client): «Pava del Baudó / Penelope ortoni», «Cangrejo fantasma rojo / Ocypode gaudichaudii», «Tortuga golfina / Lepidochelys olivacea». The catalog used to say *Gecarcinus quadratus* and *tortuga carey*, and **the crab's and turtle's paragraphs still describe THOSE species** (a land crab living in the forest; the hawksbill's overlapping scutes, curved beak and sponge diet). They are the team's text: flagged in the catalog, waiting for them to rewrite it.
- **The UI uses the POSTER's typography**: Arvo 700 for titles (like the place names JURUBIDÁ, TRIBUGÁ), Oswald 500 uppercase for animal names and buttons (like the species labels BALLENA JOROBADA), Merriweather for body text and its italic for scientific names — as `--font-title`, `--font-label`, `--font-text` in `index.html`. Before, the UI asked for "Fraunces", which was **never loaded**: phones showed Georgia. The fonts load once from the `<head>` with `media="print"` + `onload` so the stylesheet never blocks the first paint (the splash is an image and does not need them) and `display=swap` shows Georgia until they arrive; the old lazy `loadReadingFonts` is gone.
- **The first guide stays until the map is found, and after 5 s it beats.** It used to leave by itself after 5.5 s, and whoever had not got the map out yet was left looking at the camera with nothing saying what to look for. Now `ArView` pins it while `searching` and the map has never been found this session (`mapFound`); the menu moves it aside and `closeMenu` brings it back; «?» over a pinned guide leaves it pinned. After `GUIDE_CALL_TO_ACTION_MS` (5 s) it sets `#guide[data-call]` and the frame (×1.05, white glow, glowing corners), the title (×1.09) and the mission (×1.05, «4 animales» glowing gold) beat together on a 2.6 s cycle — up by 0.78 s, back by 1.6 s, then rest, the same beat-and-silence idea as the gold outlines. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` nothing moves (the desktop Chrome used for checks has it ON, so force the animation or use `getAnimations()` to inspect it). After the first find, losing the map is the bottom pill's job, not the guide's.
- **The start guide is a call to action, not just an instruction.** Under «Apunta al mapa de Nuquí» it says the mission: «y descubre los **4 animales** que se esconden en él» (the number in gold Oswald — the colour of the outlines that mark an animal). The extra line forced a third term on `#guide-frame`'s height, `calc(var(--vh) - 330px)`: without it, at 375×560 the «¿No tienes el mapa?» link (since removed on request) landed on the status pill; landscape went from 43% to 34% of the height for the same reason. `verify-ui.html` («Guía») checks it at six sizes.
- **Skinned meshes are drawn with `frustumCulled = false`.** three keeps a `SkinnedMesh`'s bounding sphere from its rest pose; when a clip moves the body away from it, three decides the mesh is off-screen and skips it — which is why the whale "just disappeared" mid-jump. `IconLoader` turns culling off for every loaded model: four models, nothing measurable saved by culling them.
- **Two animals have a small particle effect that comes out of their OWN movement** (branch `efectos-inmersivos`, three rounds with the client): when the guan moves its head and neck, leaves fall on it from above as if it brushed the branches — **6 per song** (asked for literally), none at rest; when the crab **scuttles fast** (its tap gesture), its feet kick up small sand grains that arc, settle on the ground and fade — not when it walks calmly. **The whale, the turtle and the frog have none.** Tried and dropped: bubbles on whale and turtle ("they were already perfect"), dew and then fireflies on the frog (neither convinced: "leave the frog without effects"), big faceted sand grains (looked like pebbles), sand dust clouds (too much), and a shower of leaves on the guan ("exaggerated, it spoils its animation"). The animations are untouched — the client's rule.
  - **How:** `ParticleEffect` (domain) is catalog vocabulary like `TapMove`: `kind` (`leaves`/`sand`), `from` (how the bone names END as three leaves them: `RIG_CangrejoLeftFoot_F_jnt` → `'Foot_F_jnt'`; by the end and not a substring, because rigs also carry `IK_…` and `…_main` control bones), `threshold`, `rate`, `scale` and `during` (`'always'` or `'gesture'`). `AnimalParticles` (rendering) reads those bones every frame after `MarkerPin.sync`, measures their speed in animal sizes per second (`reach × icon.scale`, like the splash) and, per bone, accumulates `(speed − threshold) × rate`; at 1 it releases `burst` particles if that bone has been quiet for `cooldown` seconds — the cooldown is what keeps it an accompaniment and not a shower.
  - **Why the crab's sand is `during: 'gesture'`:** its feet move almost as fast walking calmly (peak 1.06 sizes/s) as scuttling (1.52, and only as a spike), and even its body is 0.37 vs 0.47 — no speed threshold separates them, so "only when it scuttles fast" is expressed as "only during the tap gesture" (`MarkerPin.isGesturing`).
  - **Leaves** are lit 3D meshes (`InstancedMesh` + `MeshStandardMaterial`, curved leaf shape) born ABOVE the bone; **sand** is sprites (each with its own `SpriteMaterial` so each fades on its own; texture built as a `DataTexture`, no canvas, so tests run in node). A grain that dies on touching the ground is barely visible (a quarter-second arc): grains fly a higher arc, then SETTLE (`SETTLE_SECONDS`) and fade.
  - They live as the icon's SIBLING: in its parent (`lift` on the map, the stage or its slot in the close-up) +Y is the animal's up, so gravity is −Y in both places; when the icon changes parent the group follows and starts empty. Not emitted while the animal is still an outline; not raycastable.
  - **Calibrate** in `/verify.html`: `particleStats(true)` resets, `stepVerify(s)` advances, `particleStats()` gives per animal the bones matched, particles alive, emitted count and peak bone speed; `boneNames('crab')` lists names as three sanitizes them. **Judge visibility** in `/verify-stage.html` (count visible sprites from the console): an effect can be emitting and still be invisible — the first dust was 2–3 % of the screen at opacity 0.2–0.4 against the turquoise map.
- **`WaterSplash` reads as water because it falls, is white, and leaves ripples.** The first version was additive-blended flat dots opening in a ring: they glowed and never fell, so they read as confetti. Now droplets launch upward and come back under gravity, stretched along their velocity; a white foam base (normal blending, so it shows on the turquoise map and on a camera image alike); and two squashed rings as the water surface in perspective. It is measured in "animal sizes" and scaled by whoever shows it, so one splash serves the whale (`strength` 1) and the turtle (0.55). Both splash once, on falling INTO the water.
- **three fires ONE `loop` event when a frame wraps several loops, with `loopDelta`.** A short clip at a high `tapSpeed` (the crab's Walk used to run at 2.4) wraps twice in one long frame; counting events instead of `loopDelta` made the tap run longer than `tapLoops`.
- If a `.glb` is missing or fails to load, that model falls back to a grey disc instead of crashing the session — this is intentional, don't add a try/catch that hides the failure differently. A grey disc on screen means *check the console for `[IconLoader]`*, usually a codec the loader wasn't configured for.
- To swap an icon for a different asset: change its `source:` entry in `NUQUI_CATALOG` and drop the file in `public/models/`. Icons are modeled **+Y up** (Three.js's natural orientation); `MarkerPin` rotates the containing group 90° about X so that up becomes "out of the map".
- **`MarkerPin.dispose()` frees textures, not just materials.** `material.dispose()` deliberately leaves textures alone (three treats them as shared), so with textured `.glb`s every `clear()` would leak several MB of GPU memory without the explicit walk.
